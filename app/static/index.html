Bangla dialect, gender and age from one voice clip

A ResNet-50 and Bi-LSTM network listens to a few seconds of speech, names one of eight districts, and estimates the speaker's gender and age group. Every answer comes with a confidence and a check on whether the voice is like the ones the model learned from.

What it reports, and how well

Measured on speakers or sentences the model never trained on.

How the network listens

Cut the clip into 5-second windows

Each window becomes a log-mel spectrogram (a picture of the sound's frequencies) and a sequence of 40 MFCC values per frame.

Read it two ways

ResNet-50 reads the spectrogram as an image; a two-layer Bi-LSTM reads the MFCC sequence over time. Their two 128-number summaries are joined.

Answer three questions

District, gender and age group each have their own output. Answers for all windows are averaged, so longer clips give steadier answers.

          Results

          Every number here was measured on speakers or sentences the model never trained on, and scored the way this app scores a recording: 5-second windows, probabilities averaged. Ranges are 95% bootstrap intervals over speakers.

          How the model changed

          From the first Google Colab notebooks (v1, v2) to the Kaggle runs that followed. Each version answered one question; from V4 on, its decision rule was written before training, and where the team decided otherwise, it says so.

            Dialect

            RBVD has one speaker per district, so a model trained on it alone learns those nine voices rather than the dialects. Adding RegSpeech12, with many speakers in five districts, is what made new speakers work.

            Three districts still have one speaker

            Mymensingh, Jamalpur and Natore are only in RBVD, with one speaker each. There is no new-speaker test for them, and an answer of one of these districts for a new voice is not reliable.

            Did the same people appear in training and test?

            RegSpeech12 has no speaker labels, so its voice groups were made by clustering voice fingerprints (ECAPA embeddings). A leakage check compared every test group with its closest training group.

            Gender and age

            Gender and age were learned from Mozilla Common Voice Bangla; no gender or age label was taken from RBVD. Six models were scored on the same speakers. The extended test adds 110 new speakers to the original 109.

            Data

            Dialect comes from two regional speech corpora. Gender and age come from Mozilla Common Voice. A third corpus, BanglaAccent, is used only for testing.

            The Common Voice split for gender and age

            Clips per group. A speaker never appears in more than one split. Adult women are the rarest group: about 84% of Common Voice Bangla's training audio is male (Alam et al., 2022), and few contributors give an age of 30 or more.

            How many adult voices exist in Common Voice?

            Speakers with both age and gender labels, counted in Common Voice 17 before D2 chose its extra data. Across every Bangla pool there are fewer than 60 adult women.

            The RegSpeech12 split

            Segments per district. Whole voice groups go to one split.

            Model

            One network design, fixed since Phase I: a CNN for the spectrogram and a Bi-LSTM for the MFCC sequence, fused into three outputs. The app runs two trained copies of it.

            Two trained copies in the app

            District: GEO V4.1-A

            Trained on RBVD and RegSpeech12 after a first stage on Common Voice. Chosen because it is far better on new speakers (73.1% against 10.3%).

            Gender and age: GEO v3.1

            Trained on a balanced Common Voice split. Kept because V4.1-A was 3.6 points lower on BanglaAccent gender and 4.1 points lower on age. A model trained only for gender and age (C3) was also tested; it did not hold up on BanglaAccent, so v3.1 stays. Both copies read the same features, which are computed once.

            The combination was chosen after the V4.1 results were seen; each part's numbers were measured on the same test clips before that choice.

            Does fusing the two branches help?

            Phase-I objective 3 asked whether combining the spectral and temporal branches beats either one alone. GEO v3.1 trained each variant five times on RBVD, each time testing on sentences it had not seen.

            How V4.1-A was trained

            1. Stage 1, gender and age. Common Voice Bangla, balanced by gender and age group, split by speaker (the same split as v3.1).
            2. Stage 2, dialect. RBVD and RegSpeech12. Each epoch draws every district equally; in the five shared districts 30% of draws come from RBVD. One RegSpeech12 voice group counts for at most 60 segments. Gender and age keep seeing Common Voice clips so they are not forgotten.
            3. Choosing the epoch. 0.7 × macro-F1 on new RegSpeech12 validation speakers plus 0.3 × macro-F1 on RBVD validation. The test sets are never looked at.
            4. Calibration. A temperature for the district answer and an unknown-voice limit, both set on validation data.

            How the app scores a clip

            1. The clip is converted to 16 kHz mono. Silence at the start and end of long recordings is trimmed.
            2. It is cut into 5-second windows every 2.5 seconds; windows with less than 25% speech are left out. A clip shorter than 5 seconds is padded, as in training.
            3. Each window gets probabilities for all three questions. They are averaged over the windows.
            4. An answer below the confidence floor is marked unsure. The voice check measures how far the clip's fused summary is from each district's training data; beyond the limit the district answer is marked unknown voice.

            What the comparison shows

            • Higher numbers, easier tests

              The Bangla studies that report 85% to 95% split their data by clip or segment, so the same voices are in training and test. Studies that keep speakers or channels apart report 60% to 75% for dialect.

            • The closest match for age

              The only Bangla study with the same cut at age 30 (BRAC, 2019) reports 76% on 34 speaker decisions; its 95% range is about 60% to 88%.

            • RBVD alone cannot test new speakers

              With one speaker per district, any RBVD split tests known voices. Our own v1 scored 99.5% on a random split and V3 scored 0 to 4% on an unseen Rangpur speaker.

            • Where this project adds

              Dialect, gender and age from audio alone, tested on speakers held out from training, with a check that dialect is not just speaker identity, and with the uncertainty of each comparison reported.

            What the model cannot do yet

            • Three districts from one speaker

              Mymensingh, Jamalpur and Natore were learned from one RBVD speaker each, so answers for new speakers from these districts are not reliable.

            • Read speech in standard Bangla

              On BanglaAccent, where speakers read the same standard sentences, dialect accuracy is 9.3%, below chance. The model relies on regional words and phrasing as well as accent.

            • Age is a rough guess

              Two groups only (under 30, 30+), because Common Voice Bangla has no labelled speakers aged 60 or over and very few adult women. Balanced accuracy is about 0.60.

            • Places outside the eight districts

              The model always picks one of the eight. A voice from elsewhere gets the closest one, and the voice check flags about 44% of such recordings.

            Privacy

            A recording is sent to this server, analysed, and deleted as soon as the answer is ready. It is not stored, logged or used for training. The sample clips are from RBVD (CC BY 4.0). No BanglaAccent audio is published here, as its data agreement requires.

            For developers

            POST /api/predict with a form field audio returns the three answers as JSON, including each 5-second window's district scores. GET /api/info describes the models and their settings; GET /api/research returns the numbers behind these pages.
